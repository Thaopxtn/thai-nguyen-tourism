Overview of Thai Nguyen Province

Thai Nguyen is a strategic province in Vietnam's Northern Midlands and Mountainous Region. Following the historical merger of Thai Nguyen and Bac Kan on July 1, 2025, the new province covers approximately 8,375 km² with a population of around 1.87 million people.

Renowned nationwide for its diverse karst landscapes, rich multi-ethnic cultural heritage, and world-class green tea, Thai Nguyen also serves as a vital industrial, economic, and higher education powerhouse in northern Vietnam. Its extraordinary natural and cultural attractions offer immense potential for ecotourism, historical-cultural pilgrimage, and authentic community-based homestay tourism.

Historical Milestones & Evolution

From the ancient Vu Dinh realm of the Hung Kings to the sacred resistance safe zone (ATK) and the newly united province of 2025, Thai Nguyen has stood as a bastion of national defense and cultural resilience.

BCE - 602 CE Epoch 1

Ancient & Early Periods

During the Hung Kings era, the region belonged to Vu Dinh, one of the 15 administrative regions of the ancient Van Lang state. Under the Early Ly Dynasty (544-602), it was part of the Van Xuan state founded by Ly Bi.

Van Lang → Au Lac → Van Xuan
10th - 19th Century Epoch 2

Feudal Dynasties

Known as Phu Luong Prefecture under the Ly Dynasty, playing a vital defense shield. The name "Thai Nguyen" officially emerged in administrative records in 1397. In 1831-1832, Emperor Minh Mang established Thai Nguyen as an official province.

Phu Luong (Ly) → 1397 → Minh Mang (1831)
1891 - 1945 Epoch 3

French Colonial Period

Underwent multiple administrative adjustments: temporarily dissolved in 1891, re-established in 1892. Crucially, on April 11, 1900, the French colonial administration carved out Bac Kan Province from northern Thai Nguyen.

1892 Re-establishment → 1900 Bac Kan carved
1945 - 1954 Epoch 4

Viet Bac ATK Resistance Base

Heart of the Viet Bac revolutionary base. Dinh Hoa served as the Safe Zone (ATK), where President Ho Chi Minh and the Central Party Committee lived, operated, and directed the nation to victory in the resistance against the French.

Safe Zone ATK → President Ho Chi Minh
1954 - 2025 Epoch 5 & 6

Bac Thai & Re-establishment

1965: Merged as Bac Thai Province with the famous Thai Nguyen Iron & Steel Complex. Jan 1, 1997: Divided back into Thai Nguyen and Bac Kan. Song Cong City was established in 2015, followed by Pho Yen City in 2022.

Bac Thai (1965-1996) → Song Cong → Pho Yen
July 1, 2025 - Present The New Era

The New Thai Nguyen Province

Under Resolution No. 202/2025/QH15, Thai Nguyen and Bac Kan reunite into the new Thai Nguyen Province with 92 commune-level units. Combining southern industrial strength with Ba Be ecotourism, unlocking limitless cultural and natural tourism horizons.

Resolution 202/2025/QH15

Population & Rich Ethnic Mosaic

Demographic Profile

1.87 Million Permanent Residents

As of December 31, 2025, Thai Nguyen has 1,869,446 residents with an average population density of 223 people/km². The territory is organized into 92 commune-level administrative units (77 communes and 15 wards).

💡 Southern urban centers have higher density; northern mountain districts feature serene, forest-clad communities.
Cultural Harmony

Multi-Ethnic Coexistence

Thai Nguyen is home to vibrant ethnic groups including Kinh, Tày, Nùng, Dao, Sán Dìu, Sán Chay, and H'Mông. Each maintains distinctive customs, traditional architecture, brocade embroidery, and seasonal festivals.

Tày Nùng Dao Sán Dìu Sán Chay H'Mông Kinh
Tourism Value

Community-Based Ecotourism

Ethnic villages in the northern mountain valleys offer immersive experiences: staying in wooden stilt houses, tasting bamboo-cooked rice (Cơm Lam), listening to Then singing, and enjoying clean mountain life.

🌿 Key cultural hubs: Pac Ngoi Village (Ba Be Lake), La Bang (Dai Tu), and ATK Dinh Hoa.

Customs, Culture & Traditional Festivals

1. Traditional Customs & Stilt Houses

The customs of the Tay, Nung, Dao, Mong, and San Diu communities are tightly woven around family solidarity, ancestor veneration, and communal bonding. In highland valleys, generations still live in authentic wooden stilt houses, weave intricate brocade textiles, and preserve time-honored recipes.

✓ Living heritage preserved in ethnic homestay tourism

2. Folk Music & Performing Arts

Following the merger, northern mountain songs seamlessly harmonize with midland tea verses. UNESCO-recognized Then singing paired with the gourd lute (Đàn Tính) echoes alongside Sli, Lượn, Phong Slư, Páo Dung, and Soòng Cô singing, complemented by the lively Bát (Bowl) dance and Fan dance.

🎶 UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage: Then singing with Tinh lute

3. Vibrant Highland Festivals

Spring ignites the region with the famed Lồng Tồng (Going to the Field) festivals of Ba Be and Phu Luong, praying for bountiful harvests with Con ball throwing and buffalo plowing. Notable gatherings also include Duom Temple Festival, Nui Van - Nui Vo, and the Hich Festival.

🎯 Long Tong festival: Ritual prayers, Con ball throwing, folk games

🙏

Sacred Shrines Etiquette & Worship Protocol Guide

Essential etiquette curated by Ánh for respectful visits to Vietnamese temples & pagodas

👕 Respectful Attire

Wear sleeves covering shoulders and trousers/skirts reaching well below the knees. Remove hats and sunglasses before entering.

🚪 Proper Entrance

Enter via right gate (Right Awareness), exit via left gate (Liberation). Never step on the wooden threshold.

🍎 Pure Offerings

Present strictly vegetarian offerings (fresh flowers, ripe fruit, cakes) at Buddha altars. No meat or alcohol in main halls.

🤫 Serenity & Respect

Walk softly, speak in low tones, place donation money in official boxes, never place coins directly onto Buddha statues.
